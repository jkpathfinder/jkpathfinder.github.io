**Abstract:** Clinical gait interpretation is important to guide strategic intervention for neuromuscular disorders such as Cerebral Palsy (CP). Existing gait interpretation relies on either fixed clinical rules or population-based data-driven models to link gait observations with the underlying neuromuscular impairments, which can lead to one-size-fits-all intervention strategies with limited interpretability for a specific person. To close this gap, we introduce CausalGait, a framework that integrates clinical rules and measured gait data into personalized causal knowledge graphs, aiming to assist medical experts with more strategic intervention tailored to a specific person. Specifically, CausalGait has 3 key components: 1) Global Causal Graph, which encodes standard clinical rules into a symbolic Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG); 2) Patient-Specific Graph, which translates measured gait data into Patient-Specific Graphs via a node activation mechanism; and 3) Causal Inference and Interpretation, which performs conditional probability estimation and constrained Bayesian analysis for personalized annotation. We evaluated CausalGait on a dataset of 200 children with spastic CP collected at a credentialed gait lab. Results show that CausalGait outperforms the Data-Driven baseline on all three metrics: achieving high faithfulness (0.868), low infidelity (0.0023), and strong perturbation-based ranking consistency (normalized AOPC = 0.950). A case study further demonstrates the interpretation capability of CausalGait through medical expert analysis.
